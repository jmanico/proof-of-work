## Requirement

- **Statement**: A documented incident-response runbook MUST exist before production, defining severity classes S1–S3; naming the operator's security lead as incident commander with a second-admin deputy and privacy counsel engaged at breach determination; specifying the contain → assess → eradicate → notify → recover process, including re-application of deletion-ledger entries on any backup restore before service resumes; honoring the notification clocks per regime — GDPR 72-hour supervisory-authority notification and undue-delay individual notification when high-risk, FTC HBNR 60-day individual and FTC notification with media notice above 500 affected; and maintaining readiness through pre-drafted notification templates, an annual tabletop exercise, a written timeline with a 14-day post-incident review feeding the threat model, and automatic review of every break-glass activation as a potential incident signal.
- **Rationale**: SEC-OPS-2 states the obligation and SQ-11 fixed its content. The system holds consumer health data under multiple breach-notification regimes (SQ-1), and the threat model rates non-compliance in incident handling (TM-P-3) as high severity: without pre-assigned roles, statutory clocks, and drafted templates, a real incident consumes its notification windows on process invention. The restore step exists because a backup restore that does not re-apply deletion-ledger entries silently resurrects deleted accounts (SEC-DATA-4).
- **Assumptions**: The governing regimes are fixed (SQ-1 RESOLVED) and the detection inputs exist: SEC-LOG-4 security events and SQ-3 threshold alerts route to the security lead, and break-glass activations (SEC-OPS-1) open review items. Severity definitions per SQ-11: S1 confirmed health-data exposure, S2 suspected exposure or authentication compromise, S3 other security events. The threat model's refresh triggers (SQ-9) already include every S1/S2 post-incident review.
- **Out of Scope**: The break-glass mechanism itself (REQ-INFRA-050 — SEC-OPS-1); the deletion ledger's creation and maintenance (REQ-PRIVACY-090 — SEC-DATA-4; REQ-INFRA-040 — the log-archive account); the email channel that carries individual notifications (REQ-INFRA-060 — SEC-EXT-3); threat-model governance itself (SQ-9, resolved at the document level); the in-application pending-deletion and banner states (`DESIGN.md`, REQ-PRIVACY-110).
- **Design Traceability**: N/A — the runbook is an operational document; no `DESIGN.md` surface is introduced. (User-facing breach notices are delivered out of band by email under SEC-EXT-3, not by a designed in-product flow.)
- **Architecture Traceability**: `ARCHITECTURE.md` — trust boundaries 4 and 5 (the paths most incident classes traverse); the deletion ledger lives in the log-archive account outside the restored dataset, which is what makes the restore re-deletion step executable.
- **Security Traceability**: SEC-OPS-2 (primary); SEC-OPS-1 (break-glass auto-review input); SEC-DATA-4 (restore re-deletion is a named runbook step); SEC-LOG-4 and SQ-3 alerting (detection inputs); SQ-9 (post-incident reviews feed the threat model); SQ-1 (regime set the clocks come from).

## Security Context

- **Security Objectives**: Accountability, Privacy, Availability, Integrity
- **Control Layers**: Logging and Monitoring, Data Protection, Other — operational process
- **Threat References**: `SECURITY.md` TM-P-3 (non-compliance in incident handling — LINDDUN Nc, high severity); TM-I-8 and TM-R-2 (break-glass and below-application tampering, whose signals this process consumes); TM-P-1 (a restore without re-deletion resurrects identifiable deleted data)
- **Abuse / Misuse Case**: An incident is detected but handled ad hoc: breach determination is late, the GDPR 72-hour or HBNR 60-day clock is missed, individuals are not notified of high-risk exposure, or a backup restore silently resurrects deleted accounts because the ledger step was skipped; a break-glass activation goes unreviewed and masks insider misuse.
- **Trust Boundary**: N/A as a single boundary — the process responds to compromise at any of the six `ARCHITECTURE.md` boundaries; its own inputs arrive from boundaries 4 and 5 monitoring.
- **Untrusted Inputs or Assertions**: Initial incident reports and severity guesses (validated by assessment before determination); the presumption that a restored backup is clean (it MUST be reconciled against the deletion ledger and the incident's eradication criteria before serving traffic).
- **Authoritative Enforcement Point**: The security lead as incident commander operating the runbook; the pre-production launch gate that requires the runbook, named role holders, and templates to exist.
- **Independent Verification**: Privacy counsel independently confirms breach determination and notification obligations; the annual tabletop exercises the process against a scenario rather than trusting the document; break-glass reviews are automatic, not requested by the actor being reviewed.
- **Zero Trust Relevance**: N/A — this is an operational-response obligation, not a resource-access decision.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. **AC-01 — Runbook exists and is complete**: Given the pre-production readiness review, when the runbook is inspected, then it exists as a maintained document defining S1–S3 severity classes, naming the current security lead as incident commander, a second admin as deputy, and privacy counsel with their engagement trigger at breach determination, and specifying the contain → assess → eradicate → notify → recover process with the restore re-deletion step (SEC-DATA-4) included in recovery.
2. **AC-02 — Notification readiness**: Given the runbook's notification section, when reviewed, then it states the GDPR 72-hour supervisory-authority clock, the undue-delay individual-notification duty when high-risk, the FTC HBNR 60-day individual and FTC clock with media notice above 500 affected, and pre-drafted notification templates exist for each notice class before launch.
3. **AC-03 — Break-glass auto-review**: Given a break-glass activation (SEC-OPS-1), when it completes, then a review item is opened automatically as a potential incident signal without any manual request, and the runbook defines who reviews it and on what schedule (within 7 days per SEC-OPS-1).
4. **AC-04 — Post-incident discipline**: Given a declared S1 or S2 incident (or the tabletop standing in for one), when it closes, then a written timeline exists and a post-incident review is completed within 14 days with its findings recorded as threat-model input (SQ-9).
5. **AC-05 — Tabletop currency**: Given the launch gate and each subsequent year of operation, when readiness is audited, then a tabletop exercise record from the past 12 months exists, and the exercise validated the current role holders and the restore re-deletion step.
6. **AC-06 — Prohibited behavior**: Given any backup restore during recovery, when the restored system is brought toward service, then it MUST NOT serve traffic before deletion-ledger entries are re-applied and the re-deletion recorded; and production launch MUST NOT proceed without the runbook, named role holders, and templates in place.

## Failure Behavior

- **On Invalid Input**: A misclassified or false-positive incident report is corrected during assessment; classification changes are recorded in the timeline rather than silently rewritten.
- **On Authentication Failure**: N/A — the runbook is a process document; access to incident tooling follows the platform controls already specified.
- **On Authorization Failure**: N/A — process roles are named in the runbook; actions outside a holder's role escalate to the incident commander.
- **On Security-Decision Failure**: Default to the more protective classification: when severity or breach determination is uncertain, treat as the higher class until assessment concludes, and engage privacy counsel at determination rather than after.
- **On External Dependency Failure**: If privacy counsel or a named role holder is unavailable, the runbook's deputy chain acts; unavailability MUST NOT stop the statutory clocks, and the timeline records the substitution.
- **On System Error**: If detection inputs (alerting, break-glass review intake) fail, their failure is itself a reviewable event; incident records are kept outside the potentially compromised system where the runbook so directs.
- **Logging / Audit**: The written incident timeline: detection time, declaration, severity, determination time, notifications sent with timestamps, containment and recovery actions, restore re-deletions, and review completion. Incident records reference affected data and accounts without copying health values (SEC-LOG-3).
- **Alerting**: SEC-LOG-4 events and SQ-3 threshold alerts route to the security lead as the SEC-OPS-2 detection inputs; every break-glass activation opens a review item automatically (`SECURITY.md` SQ-11 RESOLVED).

## Implementation Notes

- **Constraints**: The runbook MUST exist before production and reflect the SQ-11 content exactly; role holders are named individuals kept current; the restore re-deletion step is part of the recovery section per SEC-DATA-4 (2026-08-03), not an appendix.
- **Prohibited Approaches**: A template runbook with unassigned roles; starting the notification clock at incident detection confusion — the GDPR clock runs from breach determination as SEC-OPS-2 states, so determination time MUST be recorded; copying health values into incident records; a recovery path that serves traffic from a restored backup before ledger re-deletion; treating break-glass review as optional or on-request.
- **Implementation Guidance**: Keep the runbook in the repository or an operator location the incident itself cannot destroy, with the named-role page as the only fast-changing part. Structure notification templates per regime and notice class (supervisory authority, individual high-risk, FTC, media) so counsel review at launch (SQ-1) covers finished drafts. Reuse the review-item mechanism REQ-INFRA-050 establishes so break-glass intake is one wiring, not a parallel system.
- **AI Development Guidance**: `REF-LOG`; `CLAUDE.md`. Drafting assistance for templates is acceptable; counsel review before launch is the mandatory gate for their content.
- **Required Human Review**: Privacy counsel review of the runbook's notification sections and templates (SQ-1 pre-launch review); security-lead sign-off on the full document; operator confirmation of role appointments.
- **Open Decisions**: None in the process content (SQ-11 RESOLVED). Statute-section mappings beyond the stated clocks remain TO BE DECIDED with SQ-1 counsel review.
